## Planned outline

1. **Two descriptions of one problem.** A closed-form model makes its assumptions explicit; a finite-element model makes fewer assumptions but hides some in the choice of element, mesh and solver.
2. **Making them comparable.** Same geometry, same boundary conditions, same constitutive law, same definition of the output quantity.
3. **Verification before validation.** Mesh convergence, load-step convergence, and comparison with a known exact solution before comparison with a new model.
4. **Reading disagreements.** When a difference points to a limitation of the model, and when it points to an error in the simulation.
5. **Reporting a comparison.** The article will propose a simple reporting format, for example:
