## Planned outline

1. **A toy model first.** The two-bar (von Mises) truss already shows a limit point and a snap. You can explore it in the [Computational Lab](/lab/von-mises-truss/).
2. **Limit points and bifurcations.** The difference between reaching a maximum of load along the equilibrium path and branching onto a new path.
3. **Shallow spherical caps.** How curvature couples bending and stretching, and why a cap under pressure can lose stability at a limit point.
4. **Imperfection sensitivity.** Why measured buckling loads of shells are often well below classical predictions.
5. **Load control and displacement control.** What a snap looks like in each case, and how numerical continuation follows the unstable part of the path.

<Callout kind="established">
For the two-bar truss with bars of initial length $L_0$, axial stiffness $EA$ and rise angle $\alpha$, exact kinematics give the apex load

$$
P(w) = 2EA\left(1-\frac{L(w)}{L_0}\right)\frac{h-w}{L(w)},\qquad L(w)=\sqrt{a^2+(h-w)^2},
$$

with half-span $a=L_0\cos\alpha$ and rise $h=L_0\sin\alpha$.
</Callout>
