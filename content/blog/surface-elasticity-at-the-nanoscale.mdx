## Planned outline

1. **Surface atoms are different.** Atoms at a free surface have fewer neighbours than atoms in the bulk. As a result, a surface can be under stress even when the body carries no external load.
2. **Why size matters.** Surface energy scales with area and bulk energy with volume, so their ratio grows as the structure shrinks. For a plate of thickness $h$, surface terms typically appear relative to bulk terms through ratios such as $E^s/(E h)$, where $E^s$ is a surface modulus with units of force per length.
3. **The Gurtin–Murdoch idea.** Model the surface as a membrane of zero thickness, bonded to the bulk, with its own residual stress $\tau_0$ and its own elastic constants.
4. **What changes in a plate.** Residual surface stress acts like an in-plane tension; surface stiffness adds to the bending and membrane rigidities. The two effects can have different signs and different consequences.
5. **Limits of the model.** Where the continuum picture has been compared with atomistic results, and where it should be used with care.
