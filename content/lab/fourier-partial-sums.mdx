## The series

The $2\pi$-periodic square wave equal to $1$ on $(0,\pi)$ and $-1$ on $(-\pi,0)$ is odd, so only sine terms appear. Its Fourier series is

$$
f(x) \sim \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k\ge 0}\frac{\sin\big((2k+1)x\big)}{2k+1}.
$$

The full derivation is worked out, in French, in the [corrected exercises on Fourier series](/analyse/).

## What to look for

<Callout kind="established">
By Dirichlet's theorem, the partial sums converge to $f(x)$ wherever $f$ is continuous, and to $0$, the average of the two one-sided limits, at the jumps. Near each jump, however, the partial sums overshoot by an amount that does not vanish as more terms are added: about 9% of the jump. This is the Gibbs phenomenon.
</Callout>

The number printed under the plot is the maximum of the partial sum computed on the plotted grid, so you can watch it settle as $N$ grows.
