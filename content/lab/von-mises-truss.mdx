## The model

Two identical elastic bars of initial length $L_0$ and axial stiffness $EA$ meet at an apex, at a rise angle $\alpha$. A vertical load $P$ pushes the apex down by $w$. With half-span $a = L_0\cos\alpha$ and initial rise $h = L_0\sin\alpha$, the current bar length is $L(w) = \sqrt{a^2 + (h-w)^2}$, the axial compressive force is $N = EA\,(L_0 - L)/L_0$, and vertical equilibrium of the apex gives

$$
P(w) = 2N\,\frac{h-w}{L} = 2EA\left(1-\frac{L(w)}{L_0}\right)\frac{h-w}{L(w)}.
$$

No small-angle approximation is made. The plot uses $L_0 = 1$, so the load is shown in units of $EA$ and the displacement in units of the rise $h$.

## What to look for

- The load first rises with $w$, reaches a **maximum** (the upper limit point), then decreases and becomes negative before returning to zero at $w = 2h$, the mirror-image configuration.
- Under a slowly increasing load, the structure cannot follow the descending branch: when $P$ reaches the upper limit load, the apex **jumps** to the far branch at the same load. That jump is snap-through.
- Shallower trusses (smaller $\alpha$) have much smaller limit loads: in the shallow limit, the limit load scales like $\alpha^3$.

## What this demonstration does not show

Dynamics of the jump, bar buckling, plasticity and imperfections are all left out. Each would change the real behaviour.
