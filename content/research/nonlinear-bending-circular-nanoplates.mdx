## The question

At the nanoscale, the surface of a plate is no longer a negligible part of the structure. Atoms at the surface sit in a different environment from those in the bulk, so the surface can carry its own residual stress and its own elastic stiffness. When such a plate bends far enough for membrane stretching to matter, both effects enter the equations of equilibrium.

This project asks how these surface effects change the nonlinear bending response of a **circular nanoplate**, and whether that response can be captured by a **closed-form** model rather than only by numerical solution.

## Approach

<Callout kind="established">
In the Gurtin–Murdoch theory, the surface is modelled as a membrane of zero thickness, perfectly bonded to the bulk, with its own constitutive law. A common linearised form is

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^s = \tau_0\,\mathbf{I} + 2(\mu_0-\tau_0)\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^s + (\lambda_0+\tau_0)\,\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^s)\,\mathbf{I} + \tau_0\,\nabla_s\mathbf{u},
$$

where $\tau_0$ is the residual surface stress and $\lambda_0,\mu_0$ are the surface Lamé constants.
</Callout>

The work combines this surface model with a nonlinear (large-deflection) plate formulation for an axisymmetric circular plate, derives a closed-form approximation of the load–deflection relation, and compares it with finite-element simulations.
